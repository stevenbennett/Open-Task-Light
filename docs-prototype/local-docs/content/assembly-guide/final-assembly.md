### Insert the counterweight

We'll start by installing the counterweight in the vertical rail.

The counterweight flex tabs go in the channel opposite the power track.

<figure>
<img src="/images/951f327ca627.jpg" alt="Finger pointing at the top of the vertical rail channel that the counterweight flex tabs slide into">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="49,23" label="68,34" match="channel opposite the power track">Flex tab channel <note>The rail slot the counterweight flex tabs slide into. It is on the side opposite the power track.</note></li>
<li dot="44,55" label="22,55">Power track <note>The brass strip runs in the channel on this side. The flex tabs go in the channel opposite it.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
{% hint style="warning" %}
The tips of the omniball pins are fragile. We're going to focus on the counterweight first, and the attached carriage can easily get tossed around. Secure the carriage somewhere while installing the counterweight to keep the pins safe.
{% endhint %}

#### Adjust the counterweight flex tabs

The flex tabs are adjustable using the M3 screw: the more you tighten the screw the tighter the fit between the counterweight and the aluminum rail. During this step we want to adjust these screws to achieve a perfect fit: loose enough that the counterweight slides effortlessly, but tight enough that it doesn't wobble or rattle.

Since you tightened these screws while [Assembling the counterweight](/open-task-light/assembly-guide/carriage-and-counterweight/assembling-the-counterweight.md), you'll probably notice that the bottom flex tab doesn't slide into the slot easily, or if it does it's very tight. Loosen the slot until the flex tab fits in the rail slot easily.

<figure>
<img src="/images/e2c17bb1d5c0.jpg" alt="Counterweight held above the vertical rail with its bottom flex tab entering the rail slot">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="46,47" label="20,47" link="Part page: self-sourcing-guide/additional-printed-parts/counterweight-flex-tab">Bottom flex tab <note>Goes into the rail slot (the channel opposite the power track) first. Loosen its screw until it slides in without forcing.</note></li>
<li dot="46,18" label="22,14" qty="1 per tab" match="M3 screw" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials">M3x10 mm screw <note>Same adjustment screw on both tabs. Tighter = snugger fit in the rail.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
From here you need to decide if the fit is too loose or too tight. Move the counterweight up and down in the rail. If you can't: it's too tight. If you can but theirs resistance: it's too tight. If you can move it freely but the counterweight doesn't stay tight to the rail: it's too loose.

What you're looking for is easy, low friction sliding with minimal wobble.

Remove the counterweight and turn the adjustment screw as needed:

Turning to the right = tighter fit

Turning to the left = looser fit

<figure>
<img src="/images/d90dc8c727e9.jpg" alt="Counterweight held sideways while a hex driver turns the flex tab adjustment screw">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="46,46" label="32,12" qty="1 per tab" match="adjustment screw" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials">M3x10 mm screw <note>The adjustment screw in the bottom flex tab, turned with the 2.5 mm hex driver. Right = tighter, left = looser. A tiny turn makes a big difference.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
After adjusting, slide the counterweight back into the rail channel.

This will take a couple of tries to dial in, and a tiny turn of the screw can make a big difference. Repeat this process until you get the bottom flex tab dialed in.

<figure><img src="/images/e2c17bb1d5c0.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Repeat this process on the top flex tab.

Continue to adjust both screws until the counterweight feels as smooth as possible. It should glide without rattling.

<figure><img src="/images/41ada2762131.jpg" alt=""></figure>

#### Insert the counterweight into the vertical rail

Once you're happy with the counterweight, gently lower it to the bottom of the vertical rail.

<figure><img src="/images/9bfba7253b0b.jpg" alt=""></figure>

### Attach the carriage

With the belt still disengaged, we're ready to install the carriage.

#### Secure the carriage on the vertical rail

Before we add the wheels, take a look inside to make sure the spacer inside isn't blocking the screw path. If it is, use a screwdriver to nudge it out of the way.

<div>
<figure>
<img src="/images/997c7981d8fa.jpg" alt="Wheel held up to show the spacer inside its bore, with the carriage and spare wheels on the table">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="48.5,53.5" label="70,30">Spacer <note>Sits inside the wheel bore between the bearings. If it has shifted, nudge it back to centre so the screw can pass through. Check every wheel before it goes on.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
<figure><img src="/images/45d6e876dc6d.jpg" alt="Screwdriver tip nudging the spacer inside a carriage wheel bore, with the carriage and spare wheels on the table"></figure>
</div>
We'll start by attaching it to the vertical rail. Align it with the vertical rail as shown.

<figure><img src="/images/cf5bce21d96f.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Insert an eccentric nut into the free hole on the backside of the carriage.

<figure><img src="/images/e9c0c01435dd.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Rotate the eccentric nut so the hole is as far as possible from the vertical rail (the 9 o'clock position).

<figure>
<img src="/images/0ebe1bc14ad1.jpg" alt="Eccentric nut in the back hole of the carriage with its off-centre hole turned away from the vertical rail">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="49,48" label="22,28" qty="1">Eccentric nut <note>Its hole is off-centre. Turn it so the hole sits as far from the vertical rail as possible before inserting the screw.</note></li>
<li dot="60,30" label="82,10">Vertical rail <note>The wheel will clamp against this rail as the nut is turned later.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Insert an M5x27 mm screw through the eccentric nut.

<figure><img src="/images/4b199a9e5955.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Slide a wheel onto the screw.

My preferred method for doing this is to push the screw back through the eccentric nut a bit until you can fit the wheel in the rail channel. Then with the wheel in place in the rail, push the screw through the wheel bearing.

<figure><img src="/images/9c82d139d64f.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/02c0e5ff9df2.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Add a lock nut and tighten the wheel using a 3 mm hex driver together with either the OTL wrench or an 8 mm wrench. Hand tighten. The wheel should be very tight, but you won't need extra leverage from an Allen key.

<figure>
<img src="/images/c0b7b9551600.jpg" alt="Hands tightening the wheel with a hex driver on one side and the OTL wrench holding the lock nut on the other">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="28,28" label="22,14" qty="Tool" link="Tools list: start-here/tools-youll-need">3 mm hex driver <note>Drives the M5x27 mm screw head. Hand tight, no extra leverage.</note></li>
<li dot="42,42" label="48,12">Wheel <note>Should end up very tight against the carriage.</note></li>
<li dot="66,41" label="84,22" qty="1" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials">Lock nut <note>Held with the OTL wrench or an 8 mm wrench while you turn the screw.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
{% hint style="danger" %}
Before you adjust the eccentric nut, go back and **tighten the other four carriage** wheels in the same way. Tighten them down good!
{% endhint %}

Use the OTL wrench or an 8 mm wrench to turn the eccentric nut in either direction, moving the wheel closer to the rail.

I like to hold the carriage with one hand while turning the eccentric nut with the other. Before you start turning you should be able to easily jiggle the carriage around, but as you turn the nut the wheels will clamp down on the rail, eliminating the jiggle. With the jiggle eliminated, move the carriage up and down the rail. It should feel butter smooth with basically no resistance.

Like the counterweight, you're trying to find a sweet spot, so it might take a couple of rounds of rotating the wheel back and forth to get it just right.

<figure><img src="/images/d459032b3b6b.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Once you've found the sweet spot on the vertical rail, we can move on to mounting the horizontal arm.

#### Attach the horizontal arm

Move the carriage to the bottom of the rail where it can safely rest. Whether you're using the freestanding or clamping base (and you used the power track offset tool) you should be able to do this without the carriage hitting the power track power connector.

<figure><img src="/images/edec5014d0a3.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Carefully balance the arm on top of the carriage wheels by placing it as shown (the front carriage wheel will be pretty close to the electronics housing).

</figure>
Insert an eccentric nut into the last remaining unoccupied hole of the carriage.

<figure>
<img src="/images/d3bd04e74a3e.jpg" alt="Eccentric nut inserted into the last open hole on the carriage above the horizontal arm">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="49,48" label="75,30" qty="1">Eccentric nut <note>The last remaining unoccupied hole. Turn it so the hole is at 12 o'clock, furthest from the arm. The two lower wheels already carry the arm; the final wheel clamps onto it from above.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Turn the eccentric nut so that the hole is as far from the horizontal rail as possible (the 12 o'clock position).

<figure><img src="/images/5349f64c619b.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Rest the final wheel on top of the arm in front of the eccentric nut.

<figure><img src="/images/66a2976635c6.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Insert the final M5x27 mm screw.

<figure><img src="/images/c168b7f752f4.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Add a lock nut and tighten the wheel using a 3 mm hex driver together with either the OTL wrench or an 8 mm wrench.

Like the other wheels, we want this very tight, but you shouldn't need any extra leverage from an Allen key.

<figure><img src="/images/359ec2803eb3.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/3e7b1832cad2.jpg" alt=""></figure>

With the wheel tightened, turn the eccentric nut until the arm is clamped between the wheels while being able to move with very little resistance.

### Engage the counterweight pulley

With the arm installed, you can safely place the belt over the pulley.

<figure>
<img src="/images/1eca6289f3c0.jpg" alt="Hand lifting the belt over the pulley at the top of the vertical rail after the arm is mounted">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="49,43" label="70,32" match="pulley" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials">GT2 idler pulley <note>Sits inside the pulley mount at the top of the rail. Only lay the belt over it once the horizontal arm is attached.</note></li>
<li dot="46,65" label="24,60" link="Belt prep: self-sourcing-guide/manual-part-preparation/gt2-timing-belt">Belt <note>Teeth face the pulley. Lift it up and over the top.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Now you'll have a chance to move the arm around and see how it feels.

* If you feel resistance in any direction, try loosening the corresponding eccentric nut.
* If you feel the carriage rattling or the horizontal arm is loose, try tightening the eccentric nut.
* The horizontal arm should feel equally easy to move up and down. If the arm is easier to move down than it is to move up, you probably need to add a bit more weight material. Use your funnel to add/remove material until it feels perfect.

For help adjusting the wheels, counterweight balance, or flex tabs, see [Movement feels stiff or uneven](/open-task-light/troubleshooting/movement-feels-stiff-or-uneven.md).
