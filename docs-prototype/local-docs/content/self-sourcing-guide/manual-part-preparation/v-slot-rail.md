# 2020 V-Slot Rail - 500 mm

Prepare each rail before starting assembly. All rail ends need to be tapped, and the horizontal rail also needs its heat pipe installed.

## Tap the rail ends

Use an M5 tap to thread every rail end. The end of the vertical rail that receives the long clamping-base screw needs at least 16 mm of thread depth. All other ends need at least 10 mm.

Mark the end with the 16 mm thread so you can identify it as the bottom of the vertical rail during assembly. Prepare it this way even if you are building the freestanding version so the rail will also work with the clamping base later.

## Prepare the horizontal rail

The heat pipe is installed in an interior channel of the horizontal rail. Assuming your heat pipe is 350 mm long, 300 mm will sit inside the rail and the remaining 50 mm will extend beyond the end.

Before gluing, use a metal scribe to make two light reference marks. Mark the heat pipe 50 mm from the end that will remain exposed, then mark the inside of the rail at the intended insertion depth: the total heat-pipe length minus 50 mm. For a 350 mm heat pipe, the rail mark should be 300 mm from the open end. These marks make it easier to confirm the heat pipe's position while the epoxy is wet.

Optionally sand the 300 mm bonding area inside the channel before applying epoxy. Removing the thin anodized layer improves adhesion. A 1/4-inch-wide, 180-grit sanding belt works well. I also like to lightly sand the gluing side of the heat pipe for a little extra adhesion. Remove all sanding dust from both surfaces before gluing.

## Install the heat pipe

Print 3–4 Heat Pipe Clamps before beginning (or find a different clamping solution that works for you)

{% hint style="info" %}
Avoid touching the heat pipe with bare hands as it can cause the copper to tarnish or become discolored over time, and/or contaminate the bonding surfaces. Wear nitrile gloves while sanding, cleaning, and installing the heat pipe whenever possible.
{% endhint %}

1. Place a small strip of masking tape over the tapped hole in the rail channel to prevent epoxy from entering the hole.
2. Apply a light strip of epoxy along the 300 mm bonding area inside the rail channel.
3. Use the reference marks to position the heat pipe so that 300 mm sits inside the channel and 50 mm extends beyond the end of the rail. Orient the squished-looking end toward the LED so that it sticks out of the rail. This end will be hidden by the LED assembly, while the cleaner-looking end will remain visible inside the rail.
4. Before clamping, move the heat pipe forward and backward, then side to side, by a few millimeters to help distribute the epoxy beneath it.
5. Space 3–4 Heat Pipe Clamps along the bonded area. The clamps are meant to keep the heat pipe flat while the epoxy cures, not to apply heavy pressure. Because the bonding surfaces are nonporous, excessive pressure will squeeze epoxy out of the sides. Tighten the clamps only enough to secure the heat pipe and keep it flat in the channel.
6. Let the epoxy cure for at least 5 hours before removing the clamps or handling the rail.
