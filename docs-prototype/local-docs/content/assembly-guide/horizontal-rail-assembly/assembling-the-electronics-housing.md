#### Insert the button sockets

Carefully insert the button sockets into the electronics housing. Start at the top of the large circular button gradually snapping it into place and working your way down towards the two square buttons.

<figure><img src="/images/bcb22aa64b2c.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Once inserted the button sockets should protrude past the top surface of the electronics housing. Check to make sure all buttons are protruding evenly.

<figure><img src="/images/68c3b46ba741.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Flip the enclosure over and double check that the bottom of the button sockets are flush with the roof of the electronics housing.

<figure><img src="/images/d83ea060a130.jpg" alt=""></figure>

#### Insert the buttons

Retrieve the three capacitive buttons from the electronics housing kit.

<li dot="44.9,58.9" label="30,86" qty="Insert 2nd" link="Part page: self-sourcing-guide/additional-printed-parts/decrease-brightness-button">Minus button <note>Square button marked with a single bar. Goes into the square socket next to the round one.</note></li>

Next we'll insert the capacitive buttons into their corresponding sockets. The order is **power button, then minus button, then plus button**.

<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="26,57" label="14,28" qty="Insert 1st" link="Part page: self-sourcing-guide/additional-printed-parts/capacitive-power-button">Power button <note>The large round capacitive button. Goes in first.</note></li>
<li dot="46,45.5" label="38,12" qty="Insert 2nd" link="Part page: self-sourcing-guide/additional-printed-parts/decrease-brightness-button">Minus button <note>The square button nearest the power button. Marked with a single bar (dim).</note></li>

Insert the buttons into the button socket. Slowly work your way around each button until the button of the button makes full contact with the bottom of the button sockets. The buttons should sit just below the socket top surface.

### Route the control board power cable

Press the cable into the control board clip by inserting one wire at a time until both wires are captured.

<figure><img src="/images/cb8ff16c85b7.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Next we'll route the cable from the control board clip, through the housing, and out the back to connect to the horizontal power track.

Bend the remaining cable into the rough shape of the cable channel.

<figure><img src="/images/fa7c7d8473bd.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Starting with the portion of the cable channel closest to the control board, use your 1.3 mm hex driver (or better yet a plastic spudger tool) to gently push the cable into the channel one wire at a time.

You'll see little retaining tabs throughout the channel. The trick is to apply pressure to the wire on either side of the tabs rather than directly on it.

<figure>
<img src="/images/57f5cd1dd160.jpg" alt="Pushing the cable into the cable channel of the housing with the tip of a 1.3 mm hex driver">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="41,43.5" label="40,25">Cable channel <note>The narrow groove along the edge of the housing arm. Start at the end nearest the control board clip.</note></li>
<li dot="78,72" label="80,57" qty="Tool" link="Tools list: start-here/tools-youll-need">1.3 mm hex driver <note>Use the tip to press one wire at a time into the channel. Press beside the retaining tabs, not on them.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
<figure><img src="/images/cf6ad7cc3f8b.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Continue by inserting the cable into the housing wall using the same technique.

<figure><img src="/images/c7b1807bec1e.jpg" alt=""></figure>

When you're finished, the cable should be snug in the enclosure and you should be able to extend and retract its free end by 2–3 cm.

<figure><img src="/images/3c180a69dfee.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/ea497eff895f.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Leave the cable in the retracted position for the next step.

<figure><img src="/images/ad6df2849d11.jpg" alt=""></figure>

{% hint style="info" %}
If you plan to leave a USB-C cable plugged in for programming, connect it to the QT Py now, before attaching the electronics housing to the rail.

Connect the QT Py to your computer using the programming cable and a USB-C cable. You should see `CIRCUITPY`. If it doesn't appear, flip the programming cable and reconnect. Still nothing? Try another USB-C cable—I've had the best luck with cables 1 meter or shorter.
{% endhint %}

#### Insert Tee Nuts

Insert two of the spring-loaded tee nuts into the side channel of the horizontal rail (either side is fine, they're identical).

Note the orientation of the tee nuts where the "long" side of the nuts are facing one another. This orientation is crucial. Space the nuts roughly as shown below, you'll have a chance to dial in the placement later.

These are "drop in" nuts so you don't need to slide them in from the end of the rail. Just insert them into the rail at a slight angle and they should snap right into place.

<figure>
<img src="/images/b62a393f31c0.jpg" alt="Two tee nuts dropped into the side channel of the rail with their long sides facing each other">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="43.3,48.3" label="44,28" qty="2 per side" match="tee nuts">Spring-loaded tee nut <note>Drop both in at a slight angle. Their long sides face each other, so the two nuts mirror one another. Spacing is rough for now; you'll fine-tune it through the housing screw holes.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Repeat on the opposite side of the rail.

<figure><img src="/images/280258c49098.jpg" alt=""></figure>

#### Install the electronics housing

With the cable connected, place the electronics housing on top of the horizontal rail.

Note the placement of the LED cable. We want it sticking out of the enclosure and resting in this gap between the lens and the LED holder. This will help us when installing the shade.

<figure>

<ol class="callouts">

<li dot="56,34.5" label="46,60">LED holder <note>The white ring around the LED. The cable sits between it and the lens.</note></li>
<li dot="58,45" label="62,58">Lens <note>The black cone under the housing.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
