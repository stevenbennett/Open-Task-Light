* **Horizontal rail** (1x)
* **LED holder with cable** (1x) (from previous step)

### Clean the surfaces

Use an alcohol wipe from the spare parts bag to wipe down both sides of the copper LED bracket, both sides of the exposed portion of the heat pipe, and both sides of the aluminum plate.

<figure><img src="/images/137e69e4501b.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/493f1b010682.jpg" alt=""></figure>

{% hint style="info" %}
You may notice that the very tip of the heat pipe separates from your rail. This is normal! The copper bracket will hold the exposed pipe securely in place.
{% endhint %}

### Install the copper bracket

#### Apply thermal paste/grease

Use your fingers to mix up the contents of a packet of thermal paste.

There are three places where you'll apply thermal paste in this section and one provides more than enough coverage. You don't need thermal past after this section, so try to keep it to one packet and save your second packet unopened and ready for future LED swaps.

This is generic thermal paste so if you have any thermal paste in a syringe (like from a CPU) feel free to use that, the syringe dispenser is often cleaner.

<figure><img src="/images/70ccd56503fe.jpg" alt="Two hands kneading a GD900 thermal paste packet in front of the horizontal rail"></figure>
Tear open the packet where indicated and apply a thin strip of thermal paste along the top of the copper bracket (the side where the bit portion sticks up). You need less thermal paste than what's shown in the image below, just a thin layer will do.

<figure>
<img src="/images/b445f538236e.jpg" alt="The copper bracket held up with a strip of thermal paste along its face, next to the exposed heat pipe tip">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="53,50" label="30,58">Thin strip of thermal paste <note>Thinner than shown here is fine; a thin, even layer is all it needs.</note></li>
<li dot="47,42" label="32,28" match="bit portion sticks up">Bit portion (sticks up) <note>The paste goes on this face of the bracket, the one this tab rises from.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
#### Attach the copper bracket

Insert the copper bracket onto the horizontal rail as shown with the plate hole aligned with the threaded hole in the rail. The goal is to slide the copper bracket onto the heat pipe without smearing the thermal paste.

<figure><img src="/images/9ae24e5f98ad.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Gently rotate the copper bracket so its hole aligns with the horizontal rail and the top makes contact with the bottom of the heat pipe. No need to push hard, you don't want to bend the heat pipe.

<figure><img src="/images/d055206f7a7b.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Use a 4 mm hex driver and the low profile M5 screw to attach the copper bracket to the rail. This screw arrives attached to the rail to secure the heat pipe protector in place, if you followed the [Prepare the rails](/open-task-light/assembly-guide/prepare-the-rails.md) guide, it should be back in your "Lighting" bag.

Hand tighten.

<figure>
<img src="/images/dceec7860a7c.jpg" alt="A 4 mm hex driver tightening the low profile M5 screw through the copper bracket into the end of the rail">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="39,44" label="45,22" qty="1" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials">Low profile M5 screw <note>Goes through the copper bracket into the threaded hole in the rail. Hand tighten only.</note></li>
<li dot="62,49" label="62,73" qty="Tool" link="Tools list: start-here/tools-youll-need">4 mm hex driver <note>Keep the driver square to the screw so you don't strip the low profile head.</note></li>

### Install the LED and LED holder

#### Prepare the LED holder

Flip the rail over.

<figure><img src="/images/04a40f589e43.jpg" alt=""></figure>

**Before proceeding**, we're going to insert the LED into the LED holder. The goal is to break this tab so the LED is easier to insert later on (the tabs tends to break anyway):

<figure><img src="/images/a4eb375ec5a7.jpg" alt=""></figure>

When you do this, make note of the contacts within the LED holder. Ultimately we'll want the C+ contact of the holder to align with the positive contact of the LED, and C- with the negative. Orientation isn't critical at this step but it helps to keep it in mind.

<figure>
<img src="/images/b2fe56a2f795.jpg" alt="Underside of the LED holder showing the square LED window with spring contacts and the two screw holes">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="43.5,46" label="60,20" match="C+ contact">C+ contacts <note>Spring contacts on the side of the window marked C+. Later these must line up with the positive pad of the LED.</note></li>
<li dot="57,49" label="76,58" match="C-">C- contacts <note>The opposite side, marked C-. Lines up with the negative pad of the LED.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
The clip should get squished and break as shown below. You can leave the tab in place when you remove the LED in the next step.

<figure><img src="/images/4ada6b89bf52.jpg" alt=""></figure>

#### Prepare the LED

Remove the LED from the LED holder.

Apply a tiny dot of thermal paste to the center of the copper bracket.

\[photo coming]

Place the LED on top of the thermal paste. Make careful note fo the orientation: the LED should sit square on the surface so that its edges aligned with the aluminum rail and the positive and negative contacts are pointing diagonally. The positive pad is closest to the rail.

Move the LED from side to side and give it a few small rotations to spread the thermal paste around. Clean up any excess thermal paste with an alcohol wipe until no thermal paste is visible.

<figure>
<img src="/images/a0d6c6fbff68.jpg" alt="The LED sitting square on the copper bracket at the end of the rail with its positive pad nearest the rail">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="49,55" label="68,44" qty="1">LED <note>Edges aligned with the aluminum rail, contacts pointing diagonally. Wiggle it to spread the paste, then wipe up any excess.</note></li>
<li dot="46.8,57.8" label="30,74" match="positive pad">Positive pad (rail side) <note>The + mark on the LED sits at the corner closest to the rail.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Grab your open task light packaging and place the horizontal rail on top of it so it the LED hangs off the edge by a few inches. This will make the next steps much easier.

<figure><img src="/images/feaa90953105.jpg" alt=""></figure>

#### Attach the LED holder

Flip the horizontal arm and apply a very thin strip of thermal past to the top of the heat pipe for later.

<figure><img src="/images/f25cd3077eb0.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Flip the horizontal arm again so the LED is facing up.

<figure><img src="/images/feaa90953105.jpg" alt=""></figure>

**Align the C+ terminal of the LED holder with the positive terminal of the LED chip, and the C- with the LED's negative terminal.** You may need to adjust the LED holder slightly until the LED finds its way into the slot.

When positioned properly the LED holder should look like this with the screw holes aligned with the screw holes of the copper bracket and the two wires aligned with the power pads on the LED.

You want to the cable to stick out towards the table so the cable connector points away from the LED.

<figure>
<img src="/images/90831fa21321.jpg" alt="The LED holder set over the LED on the copper bracket with a wire coming out on each side">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="52.7,44.5" label="60,12" qty="2">Screw holes <note>Line these up with the holes in the copper bracket.</note></li>
<li dot="55.5,47" label="78,20" match="two wires">Cable wire <note>One wire per side. Each wire should sit over the LED power pad on its side.</note></li>
<li dot="49,53" label="52,80" match="LED chip">LED <note>Adjust the holder slightly until the LED drops into the square slot.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Grab the two M3x10 mm socket head screws and place them in the holes of the LED holder. This will make it easier to align the parts in the next steps.

<figure>
<img src="/images/f5c5db80bad2.jpg" alt="An M3x10 mm screw being lowered into one of the two holes in the LED holder">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="47.5,43" label="60,22" qty="2" match="M3x10 mm socket head screws" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials">M3x10 mm screw <note>Drop one into each of the two holder holes, loose. They help align the plate and spacers in the next step.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
<figure><img src="/images/9b7a182bc6a9.jpg" alt="Both M3x10 mm screws resting loose in the LED holder holes, with the spacers and a 2.5 mm hex driver nearby"></figure>

Locate the aluminum plate and two 3 mm M5 spacers. Rest the M5 spacers on top of the aluminum plate (this plate is reversible so either orientation works).

<figure>
<img src="/images/17cb487df891.jpg" alt="The aluminum plate held up with a 3 mm M5 spacer resting on each of its two holes">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="51,41" label="66,18" qty="2" match="3 mm M5 spacers" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials">3 mm M5 spacer <note>One rests on top of the plate over each screw hole. They get sandwiched between the copper and aluminum plates.</note></li>
<li dot="46,50" label="80,44" qty="1" link="Part page: self-sourcing-guide/custom-manufactured-parts/aluminum-heat-pipe-top-plate">Aluminum plate <note>Reversible, either face up works.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
The aluminum plate will press against the underside of the heat pipe, with the spacers sandwiched between the copper and aluminum plates.

Hold the aluminum plate and spacers combo underneath the assembly with the spacers resting on top as show above. Use the M3 screws to help you align the plates and spacers.

Use a 2.5 mm hex driver to tighten the two screws. Finger tighten by alternating between the two screws, tightening a little bit at a time.

<figure><img src="/images/86212969fbd9.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Ensure there are no gaps between the LED holder, copper bracket, heat pipe, and aluminum plate. Note the orientation of the cable assembly (pointing up). Ensure the m3 screws are sticking out of the aluminum plate roughly evenly.

### Attach the lens

Locate the lens from the lighting kit.

The locking tabs on the lens are very fagile, do your best not to drop the lens because they tend to break off.

<figure>
<img src="/images/2ec5a91bcc1c.jpg" alt="The black lens held up showing its two thin locking tabs along the rim">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="40.5,39.5" label="26,24" qty="2">Locking tabs <note>One on each side of the lens rim, very fragile. They go into the slots on the LED holder, then the lens twists clockwise to lock.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Insert the lens tabs into the corresponding slots on the LED holder and rotate it clockwise until it locks into place. This takes a bit of force but the tabs can handle it.

<figure><img src="/images/0bbef0bde59c.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Your LED and cooling system are ready to go!
