# Movement feels stiff or uneven

The lamp should move easily along both rails, and the arm should feel about as easy to raise as it is to lower. If something feels off, it's usually the wheel pressure, the counterweight balance, or the fit of the counterweight flex tabs.

Unplug the barrel connector and disconnect any USB cable from its power source before making adjustments. Leave the horizontal arm installed and support it while checking the movement.

## Find which movement feels off

Try moving each axis on its own:

* **Horizontal:** Hold the carriage steady and slide the horizontal arm in and out, keeping the carriage at the same height.
* **Vertical:** Keep the arm at the same extension and move the arm and carriage up and down the vertical rail. Compare how much effort it takes in each direction, and watch the counterweight as it moves.

If one rail feels stiff in both directions, start with the wheels below. If raising the arm feels much easier than lowering it, or the other way around, check the [counterweight balance](#the-arm-feels-unbalanced). If you can see the counterweight catching, adjust the [flex tabs](#the-counterweight-gets-stuck) before changing the weight.

## The wheels are too tight against a rail

Once you've identified which rail feels stiff, use the **OTL wrench or an 8 mm wrench** to adjust the eccentric nut for that rail. This is the silver hex-shaped spacer between the wheel and the printed carriage.

1. Turn the eccentric nut a little at a time to ease the wheel's pressure against the rail. Depending on its starting position, either direction may loosen the fit. Keep the wheel's mounting screw and outer locknut secure.
2. Move along that rail again after each small adjustment.
3. Stop when movement feels easy, with no rattle or play. You want the wheels snug enough to hold the rail without squeezing it so tightly that it drags.

For a closer look at the adjustment point, see [Check carriage tightness](/open-task-light/usage/maintenance.md#check-carriage-tightness). Loosening the fit too much can also interrupt contact with the power tracks, so check for play as you go.

## The arm feels unbalanced

If the counterweight slides freely but the arm moves much more easily up than down, or down than up, the amount of weight needs adjusting.

* **Easy to move up, harder to move down:** There's too much weight in the counterweight. Remove a little material.
* **Easy to move down, harder to move up:** There's too little weight in the counterweight. Add a little material.

Use the funnel and the [counterweight assembly instructions](/open-task-light/assembly-guide/carriage-and-counterweight/assembling-the-counterweight.md#add-weight-material-to-the-counterweight-enclosure) to adjust the fill. Make small changes and refit the lid before testing the movement again. Repeat until raising and lowering the arm take about the same effort.

## The counterweight gets stuck

If you can see the counterweight catching or getting stuck in the vertical rail, its flex tabs may be too tight.

Follow [Adjust the counterweight flex tabs](/open-task-light/assembly-guide/final-assembly.md#adjust-the-counterweight-flex-tabs) for the adjustment steps and photos. Slowly loosen the **M3 screws in the flex tabs**, making small adjustments and checking the movement each time. Check both the top and bottom tabs until the counterweight slides freely without wobbling or rattling.

If you need to remove the horizontal arm to reach the tabs, **disengage the belt from the pulley first**, as shown in [Final Assembly](/open-task-light/assembly-guide/final-assembly.md#assemble-the-counterweight-pulley).

Once everything is back together, check the movement along both rails again. Reconnect power and repeat the [movement and power test](/open-task-light/assembly-guide/power-on-and-test.md#3-test-movement-and-power). If it still doesn't feel right, [let me know](/open-task-light/troubleshooting.md#still-need-help) which movement feels off and what you've already tried.
