### Assemble the bottom shell

We'll start by adding the weight material to the bottom shell and outer shell pieces.

Gather the bottom, bottom lid, the bag of steel shot (or your preferred weight material), and four M5x8 mm screws.

<figure>
<img src="/images/b19d13c5a48a.jpg" alt="The bottom shell, bottom shell lid, bag of steel shot and four M5x8 mm screws laid out">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="40,56" label="33,15" qty="1" match="the bottom" link="Print guide: printing-guide/freestanding-base/bottom-shell">Bottom shell <note>Fill it up to the inner lip where the lid sits, about 390 g of shot.</note></li>
<li dot="74,50" label="89,58" qty="1" match="bottom lid" link="Print guide: printing-guide/freestanding-base/bottom-shell-lid">Bottom shell lid <note>Goes on with the counterbored holes facing up and the tabs aligned.</note></li>
<li dot="65,28" label="65,8" qty="about 390 g used here" match="bag of steel shot" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">Weight material <note>Or your own weight material. The rest goes in the outer shell and the counterweight.</note></li>
<li dot="66,79" label="66,90" qty="4" match="M5x8 mm screws" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">M5x8 mm screw <note>3 mm hex driver. Drop them into the open holes first to keep shot out.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Fill the bottom with weight material, ensuring you don't go beyond the inner lip where the lid sits. This takes about 390 g of steel shot. The exact amount isn't critical; we just want the base to be as heavy as possible.

Take care not to get any rogue steal beads in the screw holes. You may want to plug the open screw holes with screws before pouring the weight material.

<figure><img src="/images/7e0c1de79975.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/088bd666e76f.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Place the bottom shell lid on top of the bottom piece with the screw holes aligned with the counterbored holes facing up. With the tabs aligned the lid should easily drop into place and sit below to the topmost surface of the bottom piece.

<figure><img src="/images/6c461ff3eb25.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Double check that your screw holes are free of weight material, then use the four M5x8 mm screws to secure the lid.

<figure><img src="/images/c4280313cc42.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/4e25a49a921d.jpg" alt=""></figure>

### Assemble the outer shell

Next we'll add weight to the outer shell.

Gather the prepared outer shell, outer shell lid (2x), M5x8 mm screws (2x), and the remaining weight material. If your kit came with one 1,650 g bag, keep about 500 g aside for the counterweight.

<figure>
<img src="/images/d242d65608dc.jpg" alt="The prepared outer shell, two outer shell lids, two M5x8 mm screws and the remaining bag of steel shot">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="25,42" label="25,13" qty="1" match="prepared outer shell" link="Print guide: printing-guide/freestanding-base/outer-shell">Outer shell <note>Support material already removed. Fill below the recessed surface where the lids sit.</note></li>
<li dot="53,50" label="50,84" qty="2" match="outer shell lid (2x)" link="Print guide: printing-guide/freestanding-base/outer-shell-lid">Outer shell lid <note>Counterbored holes face up. Each drops into the lid-tab slots at a slight angle.</note></li>
<li dot="59,52" label="59,24" qty="2" match="M5x8 mm screws (2x)" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">M5x8 mm screw <note>One per lid. You can plug the open holes with them while filling.</note></li>
<li dot="83,45" label="83,15" qty="keep ~500 g aside" match="remaining weight material" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">Weight material <note>Steel shot. From a single 1,650 g bag, save about 500 g for the counterweight.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Optionally plug the two open screw holes with the m5 screws.

<figure><img src="/images/24d37ea28eb5.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Fill the outer shell below the recessed surface where the lids sit. Keep the four lid-tab slots near the indicator arrows clear, and remove any beads that fall into them.

During this step you may lightly vibrate the part to encourage the weight material to settle. You can also run a (unmagnetized) screwdriver through the beads to distribute them evenly.

<figure><img src="/images/7ed4440564ce.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/1c18600e1320.jpg" alt=""></figure>

If you used the screw to plug the holes, remove them now.

<figure><img src="/images/2a77b2d7e8db.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Double check that the screw holes are free of debris. If you spot any rogue beads you can use a pair of tweezers or a magnetized screwdriver.

<figure><img src="/images/5d0e5ebc992a.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Check the four lid tab slots for debris and remove any you find using the same method.

<div>
<figure><img src="/images/f630686c9f2f.jpg" alt=""></figure>
<figure><img src="/images/84cc3047cca7.jpg" alt=""></figure>
</div>

Insert the first lid into the lid slots at a slight angle and with the counterbored holes facing up.

<figure><img src="/images/3b27ba617212.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Even at an angle you should be able to push the lid into perfect alignment.

<figure><img src="/images/1dc184b6f4b0.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Push the lid flat and use an M5x8 mm screw to secure it in place.

<figure><img src="/images/8bd5d413163f.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Repeat these steps to install the second lid on the opposite side.

<figure><img src="/images/75841cf38c4b.jpg" alt=""></figure>

{% hint style="warning" %}
With the lids secure, make sure the exposed inner surfaces of the shell are free of any beads or debris from the weight material. If a rogue piece of debris gets into the bearing, it can permanently ruin it.
{% endhint %}

Your outer shell is now a weighted outer shell.

<figure><img src="/images/380f4da25463.jpg" alt=""></figure>

While the specific weight isn't important, your filled bottom and shell pieces combined should weigh in the ballpark of 1335g.

<figure><img src="/images/9d0321ded0fb.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Set the remaining weight material aside for the counterweight assembly.

### Assemble the bracket plate

Continue by gathering the bracket plate, four M5x12 mm screws, four M5 square nuts, and the turntable bearing.

<figure>
<img src="/images/7838a0c6face.jpg" alt="The bracket plate, turntable bearing, four M5x12 mm screws and four M5 square nuts laid out">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="26,35" label="31,12" qty="1" link="Print guide: printing-guide/freestanding-base/bracket-plate">Bracket plate <note>The four square pockets take the square nuts, text side down.</note></li>
<li dot="60,58" label="62,72" qty="1" link="Bearing prep: self-sourcing-guide/manual-part-preparation/turntable-bearing">Turntable bearing <note>Test both orientations and keep the smoothest-spinning side as the top.</note></li>
<li dot="31,64" label="50,64" qty="4" match="M5x12 mm screws" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">M5x12 mm screw <note>Thread all four in loosely, then tighten evenly around the circle.</note></li>
<li dot="31,75" label="50,76" qty="4" match="M5 square nuts" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">M5 square nut <note>Insert with the text side down for the best thread engagement.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
The base bearing can be installed in either orientation, but they often have an optimal orientation. Test both sides of your bearings by holding the outer piece with one hand and spinning the inner piece with the other. Then flip it over and test the other side. Keep the bearing in the orientation with the smoothest rotation (in the case of the photo below, if this orientation produced the smoothest rotation, the side facing the camera would become the "top" onto which we'll install the bracket plate in the following step).

<figure><img src="/images/7921bf1494ea.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Next we'll prepare the bracket plate. It will sit on top of the bearing like this:

<figure><img src="/images/89cc5ab06fde.jpg" alt=""></figure>

We're going to insert our square nuts into the bracket plate. You'll find text on one side of each square nut. For optimal thread engagement insert the nuts with the **text side down.**

<figure><img src="/images/ac82619459f2.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/b19367a58160.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Repeat for the remaining three square nuts.

<figure><img src="/images/72a66a070931.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Place the bracket plate on top of the bearing with the inner bearing holes aligned with the square nuts. Thread in the first M5x12 mm screw without tightening it.

<figure><img src="/images/619d3bf07f83.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Thread in the screw opposite the first one (just thread it in for now).

<figure><img src="/images/b4f0f47d4dd2.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Thread in the remaining two screws. With all of the screws in place, tighten them evenly by working your way around the circle, tightening each screw a little at a time until they're all hand-tight.

<figure><img src="/images/228b72cc7cc4.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Gather your bag of corner brackets and three M5x10 mm screws. These corner brackets will connect the vertical rail to the base.

<figure>
<img src="/images/50ed97288b4a.jpg" alt="The bearing and bracket plate assembly beside the corner brackets bag and three M5x10 mm screws">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="22,55" label="22,24" qty="1 bag" match="bag of corner brackets" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">Corner brackets bag <note>Holds three corner brackets and three M5x5 mm set screws.</note></li>
<li dot="73,50" label="86,50" qty="3" match="M5x10 mm screws" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">M5x10 mm screw <note>Secure the brackets to the bracket plate. Only about 90% tight so the brackets can still rotate.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Take out the corner brackets and M5x5 mm set screws (three of each).

<figure><img src="/images/c92dd9d1f77a.jpg" alt=""></figure>

We're going to insert the set screws to guarantee the correct orientation in the next steps.

The two arms of the brackets are slightly different. One has cutouts near the corner while the other has a consistent width throughout. The set screw goes in the latter (the arm without the cutout).

<figure><img src="/images/384b4a54e531.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/e0af7be3a089.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Thread the set screw into the threaded hole of the non-cutout arm. The screw's socket should face the inside of the bracket as shown below.

<figure><img src="/images/970bfbb2e95f.jpg" alt=""></figure>

You don't need to tighten the screw completely, the goal is to just store it in the correct hole.

<figure><img src="/images/67d2474d2c88.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Repeat for the remaining two corner brackets.

<figure><img src="/images/5db694fa3b44.jpg" alt=""></figure>

You're going to attach these brackets at three of the four mounting holes on the bracket plate. Note the geometry near the fourth hole. **Don't put a corner bracket here.**

<figure>
<img src="/images/707b3d9e7759.jpg" alt="The bracket plate on the bearing with a driver pointing at the cable cutout beside the fourth mounting hole">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="64,41" label="82,20" qty="Leave empty" match="fourth hole">Fourth mounting hole <note>Don't put a corner bracket here. The square nut next to the cable cutout stays free. The power jack plate cable passes through here.</note></li>
<li dot="52,47" label="86,66" match="geometry">Cable cutout <note>The notch in the centre hole. The bracket-free nut is the one nearest this notch.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Arrange the brackets as shown with the empty threaded hole close to the printed part and end with the set screw pointing up.

<figure><img src="/images/945fca054929.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Use the M5x10 mm screws to secure the brackets to the bracket plate. We want these loose enough that they can rotate easily, so tighten them about 90% of the way.

<figure><img src="/images/6428984da1a6.jpg" alt=""></figure>

When you're done they should rotate a little. This will help us slide the vertical rail on later which will align the brackets perfectly for us.

### Install the vertical rail

Along with your partially assembled base, gather your vertical rail (the one without the heat pipe), the bracket cover, and an M5x10 mm screw.

You'll also need both a 3 mm and 2.5 mm hex driver.

<figure>
<img src="/images/4d00cd30f973.jpg" alt="The partially assembled base, vertical rail, bracket cover, M5x10 mm screw and the 3 mm and 2.5 mm hex drivers laid out">
<ol class="callouts">
<li dot="50,27" label="50,12" qty="1" match="vertical rail (the one without the heat pipe)" link="Rail prep: self-sourcing-guide/manual-part-preparation/v-slot-rail">Vertical rail <note>The marked, extra-long-tapped end is the bottom. It slides onto the corner brackets.</note></li>
<li dot="57,54" label="60,38" qty="1" link="Print guide: printing-guide/freestanding-base/bracket-cover">Bracket cover <note>The single hole on its underside lines up with the hole in the base.</note></li>
<li dot="62,65" label="62,76" qty="1" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">M5x10 mm screw <note>Secures the bracket cover. Finger tight.</note></li>
<li dot="76,55" label="79,92" qty="Tools" match="3 mm and 2.5 mm hex driver" link="Tools list: start-here/tools-youll-need">3 mm and 2.5 mm hex drivers <note>3 mm for the bracket screws, 2.5 mm for the M5x5 mm set screws.</note></li>
<li dot="33,75" label="33,91" qty="1">Partially assembled base <note>Outer shell with the bearing assembly installed.</note></li>

Locate the marked end of your vertical rail. This end is tapped extra long to accommodate the long screw used in the clamping base. Even though we're building the freestanding base, it's a good idea to designate this end as the "bottom" of your vertical rail in case you decide to swap bases in the future.

<figure><img src="/images/6f71f46b0089.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Slide the bottom of your vertical rail onto the corner brackets. With the bracket screws slightly loose you should be able to easily wiggle the vertical rail into place. If you can't, try loosening the screws a bit.

<figure><img src="/images/a56f46163076.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Ensure that the rail is sitting flat on the base with one hand, and use a 2.5 mm hex driver to lightly tighten the first M5x5 mm set screw.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Don't fully tighten this screw yet.
{% endhint %}

<figure><img src="/images/17cb65b1c3ff.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Slightly tighten the set screw on the opposite side of the rail.

Finally, slightly tighten the third screw (perpendicular from the first two).

<figure><img src="/images/ad95e423f24b.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Repeat this sequence, tightening each screw a little at a time until they're hand-tight (we'll tighten them fully later).

<figure><img src="/images/d595c3c39515.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Maneuver the power cable so it's hidden within the rail channel.

<figure><img src="/images/4c1175ac551f.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Locate your bracket cover and find the single hole on the underside. This hole should align with the hole in the base when slid into place.

<figure><img src="/images/0a5025ff30f3.jpg" alt=""></figure>

With the hole aligned, flip the cover over and slide it down from the top of the vertical rail.

<figure><img src="/images/ef75993599c5.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/ec73e93f388e.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/df0d875c73a7.jpg" alt=""></figure>

<figure><img src="/images/91b7800a4573.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Lay the lamp on its side and use the M5x10 mm screw to secure the bracket cover into place. Finger tighten.

<figure><img src="/images/fb8debb8247a.jpg" alt=""></figure>

With the rail and bracket cover in place we can fully tighten the three screws securing the corner brackets. These need to be fully tightened so it's best to use a tool which can generate some leverage like an Allen key. Fully tighten (within reason, at a certain point you'll start to compress the printed part).

<figure><img src="/images/130d30817cea.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Flip the lamp right-side-up and use a 2.5 mm Allen key to fully tighten the corner brackets in the rail, tightening each screw a partial turn at a time until all three are fully tightened.

{% hint style="danger" %}
Take care when tightening the set screws. While they do need to be quite tight, you really don't want to strip the hex socket. Make sure your Allen key is fully inserted during tightening and try to keep it nice and straight.
{% endhint %}

<figure><img src="/images/0dda6fb5540d.jpg" alt=""></figure>

### Attach the bottom shell

Flip the lamp back on its side and gather your weight-filled bottom shell and the remaining two 27 mm screws.

<figure>
<img src="/images/a5efbeb7bc23.jpg" alt="The weight-filled bottom shell, two M5x27 mm screws and the base with the vertical rail lying on its side">

<li dot="30,56" label="25,78" qty="1" match="weight-filled bottom shell" link="Print guide: printing-guide/freestanding-base/bottom-shell">Bottom shell <note>Arrows on its underside line up with the arrows on the outer shell rim.</note></li>
<li dot="48,75" label="48,88" qty="2" match="27 mm screws" link="BOM: self-sourcing-guide/bill-of-materials/bom-freestanding">M5x27 mm screw <note>The long ones. They go through the bottom shell into the base.</note></li>
</ol>
</figure>
Note the location of the two indicator arrows on the perimeter of the outer shell.

<figure><img src="/images/ca8684317703.jpg" alt=""></figure>

You'll find corresponding arrows on the underside of the bottom shell.

<figure><img src="/images/0f3b74f669cf.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Place the bottom shell onto the rest of the lamp with the arrows aligned (the bottom is functionally symmetrical so as long as both arrows are pointing at an arrow on the base you're good to go).

<figure><img src="/images/fd9ba6691471.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Secure the bottom shell to the base using the two M5x27 mm screws.

<figure><img src="/images/f25916d807e5.jpg" alt=""></figure>

### Insert the power cable

Locate the power extension cable found in the freestanding base kit.

<figure><img src="/images/b35da00f480d.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Insert the barrel into the power jack at the center of the base.

<figure><img src="/images/4114ca9cb4a7.jpg" alt=""></figure>

Use your fingers to push the first few inches of the extension cord into the channel until the cable is fully submerged and the base can sit flat.
